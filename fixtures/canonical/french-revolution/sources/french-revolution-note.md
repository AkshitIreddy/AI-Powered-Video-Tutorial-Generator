# French Revolution: evidence-oriented teaching note

This locally authored fixture note summarizes a narrow 1789 sequence for testing timelines, maps, qualification, and source credits.

- Louis XVI convoked the Estates-General at Versailles in May 1789 amid a fiscal and political crisis.
- Representatives of the Third Estate declared themselves the National Assembly in June.
- The storming of the Bastille in Paris on 14 July became a powerful symbol of the Revolution.
- The National Constituent Assembly adopted the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen in August 1789.
- In October, the Women's March on Versailles helped force the royal family to relocate to Paris.

The events had multiple interacting causes, including state fiscal problems, institutional conflict, unequal privileges, food-price pressure, and Enlightenment political ideas. A tutorial must not describe any single factor as the sole cause.

The Declaration articulated universal rights language while political participation and practical inclusion remained contested and unequal. The fixture distinguishes the text's claims from later historical outcomes.
